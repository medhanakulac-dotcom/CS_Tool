05How playbooks are pickedtwo inputs · tap to expand
Input 1 · Commercial segment

How valuable an account is to seatOS — based on contract value, growth potential and strategic fit. It answers “how much should we invest here?” and sets the priority and cadence of the motion. Segment is set commercially and reviewed each quarter; it doesn't change week to week.

High

Strategic, top-value accounts — largest contracts or strongest growth and reference potential. They earn the most hands-on attention and the fastest cadence, and we actively push them toward full adoption.

Mid

Solid, mid-value accounts worth proactive care, but on a lighter touch than High. Three active features is treated as an acceptable baseline rather than a problem to fix.

Low

Smaller accounts handled mostly through automated, scalable motions — activation journeys, nudges and self-service — so the team's time stays focused on higher-value segments.

Dormant

Inactive or at-risk accounts with no live engagement. Reactive only — we respond to inbound requests but run no proactive outreach until they re-engage.

Input 2 · Product health — by Weekly Active Operators

A simple, objective score: count how many of the six core seatOS features have a Weekly Active Operator — a real user driving that feature each week. That feature count maps directly to a health state and what it means commercially.

FEATURE COUNTPRODUCT HEALTHCOMMERCIAL MEANING
5–6HealthyStrong adoption
3–4AdoptedAcceptable / usable
0–2UnhealthyLow adoption
WHY “ADOPTED” MEANS DIFFERENT THINGS BY SEGMENT
We recognize 3-feature adoption as an acceptable Product Health baseline — but Commercial Segment determines the level of ambition. For a High-value account, 3 features means the account is usable, but not yet strategic enough for growth, so its playbook keeps pushing toward 5–6 features. Mid and Low accounts, by contrast, are treated as acceptable at 3+ features and shift into maintain / self-serve motions.
